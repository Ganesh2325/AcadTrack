Student assignment and academic progress

The assignment record for a whole department.

AcadTrack is a local Django system for publishing coursework, collecting submissions, requesting revisions, and keeping marks attached to the attempt they belong to. Students, faculty, and administrators each see only the work they are allowed to touch.

Three roles Drafts stay private Every attempt is kept Runs on Django and SQLite

Why it exists

Coursework should not live in inboxes.

When briefs, files, and comments are scattered, deadlines slip and a resubmission replaces the original. AcadTrack keeps one record for the life of an assignment.

What the department gets

  • Subjects with faculty ownership and enrollments that cannot be duplicated.
  • Assignments saved as drafts, published to a class, or limited to selected students.
  • Deadlines, maximum marks, late-submission permission, and resubmission permission.
  • A submission list that shows who has turned work in and who has not.

What stays true in the data

  • One submission record per student and assignment.
  • Each attempt stores its own text, file, time, and late flag.
  • Marks and comments belong to that attempt, not to a single overwritten file.
  • A late submission is never shown as if the student never sent anything.

Assignment lifecycle

From a faculty draft to a completed mark.

1

Draft

Faculty write the brief on a subject they teach. Students cannot open a draft.

2

Publish

A new assignment needs a future deadline. It can go to every active enrollment or a chosen list.

3

Submit

Students send text, an allowed file, or both. The clock records whether the work is late.

4

Review

Faculty read the latest attempt, mark it under review, and enter marks that cannot exceed the maximum.

5

Revise

A revision request opens one more attempt only when the assignment allows resubmission.

6

Complete

Acceptance closes the work. Earlier attempts and their feedback remain available.

Who uses it

Access is decided on the server, not by hiding a button.

Students

Public registration creates a student account only.

  • Personal dashboard, deadlines, and subject progress.
  • Search and filter assigned work.
  • Submit, read feedback, and resubmit when a revision is open.
  • Profile edits for name, email, department, and semester. Roll number and role stay fixed.

Faculty

An administrator adds a person to the Faculty group.

  • Subjects they teach, with enrollment control.
  • Create, publish, archive, or delete an assignment they own.
  • See eligible students, including those who have not submitted.
  • Evaluate only their own assignments.

Administrators

Django Admin remains the institutional desk.

  • Users, groups, and permissions.
  • Subjects, enrollments, assignments, and evaluations.
  • Promotion from student access to faculty access.
  • Historical attempt fields stay protected from casual edits.

Inside the product

The same system behind the dashboards.

Subjects

Codes, descriptions, faculty membership, and active or inactive enrollment.

Assignments

Title, instructions, reference file, deadline, marks, audience, and publication state.

Submissions

PDF, Office, text, zip, and common source files up to 5 MB. Files are stored, never executed.

Evaluation

Marks, comments, accept, or request a revision. The decision is tied to one attempt.

Progress

Counts come from eligible assignments only. Drafts and other students’ private work are left out.

Preference

Light or dark appearance is saved in a cookie. Sign-in itself uses the Django session.

Trust

Private academic work stays with the people who own it.

Sign-in

Username or email, Django password hashing, and a session that ends on logout.

Boundaries

Students cannot open faculty tools. One student cannot download another student’s file. Faculty cannot mark a colleague’s assignment.

Files

Downloads pass through an authorization check. Uploaded code is never run by the server.
